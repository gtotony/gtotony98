On the Quantitative Weight Dependences of Vector-Valued Multilinear Fractional Operators

The multilinear fractional integral operator $\mathcal{I}_\alpha$, introduced by Kenig and Stein, maps $m$-tuples of locally integrable functions into a target Lebesgue space governed by the multilinear Sobolev homogeneity relation $\frac{1}{p} = \sum_{i=1}^{m} \frac{1}{p_i} - \frac{\alpha}{n}$. Within the one-weight Muckenhoupt paradigm, the class $A_{\vec{P},p}$ of Moen supplies the weight condition adapted to this off-diagonal scaling. A structural gap has nevertheless persisted: the multi-indexed, off-diagonal vector-valued norm inequalities for $\mathcal{I}_\alpha$ had remained conceptually unquantified, since the available formulations relied on qualitative limiting techniques that cede control over the growth rate of the operator norm in the weight characteristic. In this work, published in Journal of Inequalities and Applications (2026), we close this gap by tracking that growth explicitly.

The mechanism proceeds in two separated stages. We first discretize the continuous multi-indexed potential kernel onto dyadic annuli, which circumscribes $\mathcal{I}_\alpha$ by localized dyadic averages over a sparse family. This reduction is deterministic: every geometric constant generated by the passage from the continuum to the dyadic grid is carried forward in closed form.

Theorem 5 (Pointwise Sparse Domination for Multilinear Fractional Integrals)

Let $0 < \alpha < mn$ and let $\mathcal{I}_\alpha$ act on $\vec{f} = (f_1,\dots,f_m)$. For every vector of functions with bounded support there exists a sparse family $\mathcal{S}$ such that, for almost every $x \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $$|\mathcal{I}_\alpha(\vec{f})(x)| \le C_{n,m,\alpha} \sum_{Q \in \mathcal{S}} |Q|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}} \Big( \prod_{i=1}^{m} \langle |f_i| \rangle_{Q,1} \Big) \chi_Q(x),$$ where the constant is resolved explicitly as $C_{n,m,\alpha} = \big(2^{mn-\alpha} 6^{mn}\big)\big(2^{m} C_{\text{weak}}^{m} 6^{mn}\big)\big(1-2^{-\alpha}\big)^{-1} 6^{mn}$, and the resulting family is $\eta$-sparse with $\eta = 2^{-(n+1)} 36^{-n}$. The dependence on the fractional exponent $\alpha$ is thereby tracked rather than absorbed.

The second stage lifts this scalar localization to multi-indexed sequence spaces. A direct analysis of vector-valued sparse operators under sequence dualization meets topological obstructions — non-convexity in the lower sequence regimes and instability at the $L^1$ endpoint. We bypass these by a decoupling scheme: an explicit scalar weighted bound for the sparse operator within the LMS-range $\max_i p_i' \le p(1-\alpha/n)$, followed by the off-diagonal extrapolation mechanism of Cao, Olivo and Yabuta, specialized to the exact multilinear Sobolev parameters. The weight-characteristic function transfers to the $\ell^r/\ell^{r_i}$ setting without modification.

Theorem 9 (Quantitative Vector-Valued Multilinear Inequalities)

Fix $0 < \alpha < mn$ and exponents $1 < p_1,\dots,p_m < \infty$ satisfying the multilinear Sobolev homogeneity relation with $p \ge 1$. Impose the structural condition $\max_i p_i' \le p(1-\alpha/n)$, let $\vec{w} \in A_{\vec{P},p}$, and fix an inner aggregation tuple $\vec{r} \in \mathcal{R}_\alpha$. Then $$\Big\| \Big( \sum_k |\mathcal{I}_\alpha(f_{1,k},\dots,f_{m,k})|^r \Big)^{1/r} \Big\|_{L^p_{\nu_{\vec{w}}}} \le C\, [\vec{w}]_{A_{\vec{P},p}}^{\tilde{\kappa}} \prod_{i=1}^{m} \Big\| \Big( \sum_k |f_{i,k}|^{r_i} \Big)^{1/r_i} \Big\|_{L^{p_i}_{w_i}},$$ with explicit exponent $\tilde{\kappa} = p\left(1 - \frac{\alpha}{n}\right)$, where $\frac{1}{r} = \sum_{i=1}^{m} \frac{1}{r_i}$ and $C = C_{n,m,\vec{P},\alpha,\vec{r}}$ is deterministic in $\vec{w}$.

Key Takeaway: Quantitative rather than Qualitative

The entire weight-dependence of the estimate is concentrated in the single factor $[\vec{w}]_{A_{\vec{P},p}}^{\tilde{\kappa}}$; the residual constant is deterministic in $\vec{w}$. The estimate therefore exhibits in closed form the analytic rate at which the operator norm degrades as the weight characteristic grows, in contrast to an exponent accessible only as the unquantified output of an abstract limiting procedure. A single inequality governs the scalar and vector-valued regimes at once: restricting the sequence to one non-zero tuple collapses the inner aggregations and returns the scalar weighted bound, so the vector-valued extension is obtained at no cost in the weight exponent. Within the range delimited by the structural condition and the fractional positivity condition, $\tilde{\kappa}$ is on par with the scalar benchmark of Lacey, Moen, Pérez and Torres after conversion between the un-renormalized and renormalized weight conventions.

The borderline regime $p \to 1$ and weak-type endpoint estimates lie outside the present range and are not addressed by this method. The analysis is moreover carried out for the standard multilinear fractional kernel; an extension to rough fractional kernels would proceed by replacing the discretization step with a sparse domination adapted to the weaker kernel hypotheses, after which the extrapolation mechanism transfers without modification. The complete proofs and explicit constant derivations are published open-access in Journal of Inequalities and Applications: DOI: 10.1186/s13660-026-03511-x

Explicit Approximation Rates for Shallow Neural Networks on Additive Besov Classes: Breaking the Curse of Dimensionality

In mathematical machine learning and constructive approximation theory, a central challenge is overcoming the curse of dimensionality. When approximating functions on bounded Lipschitz domains $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ using single-hidden-layer neural networks, classical Jackson estimates on the Besov scale $B^s_{p,q}(\Omega)$ yield error decay rates of order $N^{-s/d}$ with network width $N$. As ambient dimension $d$ grows, this convergence rate rapidly degrades. In our recent work published in Mathematics (2026, 14(14), 2491), we establish explicit, non-asymptotic approximation error bounds for localized shallow networks, prove a univariate inverse Bernstein converse on nonlinear free-knot Besov spaces, and demonstrate that on coordinate-subset additive Besov classes $\mathcal{S}^s_{d_0}(\Omega)$, the approximation rate is governed solely by the effective dimension $d_0$, with the ambient dimension $d$ entering only through the explicit prefactor constant.

Theorem 5 (Direct Jackson-Type Estimate for Localized Shallow Networks)

Let $\Omega \subset \mathbb{R}^d$ be a bounded Lipschitz domain. Let $\sigma$ satisfy standing regularity hypotheses (A1)–(A3) and admit an exact spline generator $\Lambda \in \mathcal{N}_{\nu_0}(\sigma; 1)$ of order $W \ge r_\sigma + 1$ reproducing polynomials of degree $\le r_\sigma$. For $1 \le p \le \infty$, $0 < q \le \infty$, and $0 < s < s_\sigma = r_\sigma + 1$: $$E_N^\flat(f)_{L_p(\Omega)} \le C_J N^{-s/d} |f|_{B^s_{p,q}(\Omega)}, \quad \forall N \ge N_0$$ where $C_J = C_J(\sigma, s, p, q, d, \Omega) > 0$ is explicitly tracked through the activation parameters $(K_\sigma, m_\sigma, r_\sigma, \nu_0, W)$ and the Lipschitz extension constant $C_W(\Omega)$ via the homogeneous $K$-functional $K(f, t^r; L_p, \dot{W}^r_p) \asymp \omega_r(f, t)_{L_p}$.

In one dimension ($d=1$), we establish the corresponding converse theorem on the nonlinear diagonal Besov scale, characterizing the approximation space of localized neural networks:

Corollary 1 (Univariate Converse / Inverse Bernstein Estimate on $B^\alpha_{\tau,\tau}$)

Let $d=1, \Omega=(0,1)$ and $\sigma = \text{ReLU}^k$ ($s_\sigma = k+1$). If $f \in L_p(\Omega)$ ($1 \le p < \infty$) satisfies the dyadic $\ell_\tau$-summability condition for $0 < \alpha < s_\sigma$ and $\frac{1}{\tau} = \alpha + \frac{1}{p}$: $$|f|_{\mathcal{A}} := \left[ \sum_{j \ge 0} \left( 2^{j\alpha} E_{2^j}^\flat(f)_{L_p(\Omega)} \right)^\tau \right]^{1/\tau} \le A < \infty$$ then $f \in B^\alpha_{\tau,\tau}(\Omega)$ on the nonlinear free-knot Besov scale, and $\|f\|_{B^\alpha_{\tau,\tau}(\Omega)} \le C' (\|f\|_{L_p(\Omega)} + A)$ with explicit $C' = C'(k, \alpha, p)$.

The principal breakthrough of this work is the effective-dimension refinement. On a bounded product domain $\Omega = \prod_{i=1}^d \Omega_i$, functions in the coordinate-subset additive Besov class $\mathcal{S}^s_{d_0}(\Omega) = \{f(x) = \sum_{|T|=d_0} g_T(x_T) : g_T \in B^s_{p,q}(\Omega_T)\}$ carry $d_0$-variate coordinate interactions ($1 \le d_0 \le d$):

Theorem 6 (Effective-Dimension Refinement on Additive Besov Classes)

For every $f \in \mathcal{S}^s_{d_0}(\Omega)$ and $N \ge N_0''$: $$E_N^\flat(f)_{L_p(\Omega)} \le C'' N^{-s/d_0} |f|_{\mathcal{S}^s_{d_0}(\Omega)}$$ The decay exponent $-s/d_0$ depends strictly on the effective dimension $d_0$. The ambient dimension $d$ enters only through the explicit prefactor constant $C'' = \Lambda C_1 (2\kappa)^{s/d_0} \binom{d}{d_0}^{s/d_0}$, where $\kappa = \frac{\nu' + d\nu''}{\nu' + d_0\nu''}$ is the localized coordinate embedding factor and $\Lambda$ is the geometric fiber measure bound.

Key Takeaway & Interpretability in Applied Learning

By distributing the width budget across coordinate-subset faces via deterministic least-squares projections, we achieve dimensional rate decoupling without iterative optimization variance. On educational tabular datasets (UCI Student Performance), held-out $L_2$ diagnostics confirm that target prediction is governed by effective dimension $d_0 = 1$, validating that feature-parsimonious, explainable additive architectures prevent overfitting to spurious high-order coordinate noise.

The complete proofs, explicit constant derivations, and empirical validation scripts are published open-access in Mathematics: DOI: 10.3390/math14142491

Explicit Wavelet Approximation in Weighted Besov Spaces: Resolving Structural Breaks in Piecewise Regular Series

Constructive approximation theory often establishes asymptotic convergence orders without displaying explicit constants. In our recent paper published in Mathematics (2026, 14(15), 2794), we construct explicit direct (Jackson) and inverse (Bernstein) multiresolution approximation estimates in weighted Besov spaces $B^s_{p,q}(w)$ over Muckenhoupt $A_p$ weights, routing the entire weight dependence through the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator norm $C_M(p, [w]_{A_p})$.

Theorem 1 (Direct Jackson Estimate) & Theorem 2 (Inverse Bernstein Estimate)

Let $1 < p < \infty$, $1 \le q \le \infty$, $w \in A_p$, and $0 < s < \sigma = \min(M, \gamma)$. For the biorthogonal projection $P_J$ onto $V_J$: $$\|f - P_J f\|_{L^p(w)} \le C(s,q) C_{\text{ch}} 2^{-Js} \|f\|_{B^s_{p,q}(w)}, \quad \forall f \in B^s_{p,q}(w)$$ $$\|f_J\|_{B^s_{p,q}(w)} \le C' 2^{Js} \|f_J\|_{L^p(w)}, \quad \forall f_J \in V_J$$ Every factor is in closed form or bounded by $C_M(p, [w]_{A_p}) \le c_p [w]_{A_p}^{1/(p-1)}$.

For piecewise regular series $f = g + h \in \mathcal{P}^s_K(w)$ carrying a Besov-smooth trend $g$ and isolated structural break jumps $h = \sum_{i=1}^K c_i \mathbf{1}_{\{x \ge x_i^0\}}$ with $\sum c_i = 0$, the projection error splits analytically:

Theorem 3 (Piecewise Regular Decomposition & Effective Rate)

$$\|f - P_J f\|_{L^p(w)} \le C_{A1} 2^{-Js} \|g\|_{B^s_{p,q}(w)} + C_\phi \sum_{i=1}^K |c_i| w(I_J(x_i^0))^{1/p}$$ where $I_J(x_i^0) = [x_i^0 - 2A 2^{-J}, x_i^0 + 2A 2^{-J}]$. When $w$ is locally comparable to Lebesgue measure, the effective convergence rate is exactly: $$\text{Effective Decay Rate} = \min\left(s, \frac{1}{p}\right)$$

On the Geometric Phase-Space Obstructions in Wavelet Frames: Understanding the Anisotropic Balian-Low Theorem

In time-frequency analysis and multiscale representation theory, a fundamental tension exists between the smoothness of a generator window and its phase-space localization. In classical Gabor analysis (Weyl-Heisenberg systems), this limitation is famously codified by the Balian-Low Theorem (BLT).

Classical Balian-Low Theorem (Gabor Case)

Let $g \in L^2(\mathbb{R})$ and consider the Gabor system $\mathcal{G}(g, \alpha, \beta) = \{e^{2\pi i m \beta x} g(x - n \alpha)\}_{m,n \in \mathbb{Z}}$. If $\alpha \beta = 1$ and $\mathcal{G}(g, \alpha, \beta)$ forms a Riesz basis for $L^2(\mathbb{R})$, then: $$\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}} |x g(x)|^2 dx\right) \left(\int_{\mathbb{R}} |\xi \hat{g}(\xi)|^2 d\xi\right) = +\infty$$

That is, a Riesz basis of Gabor atoms cannot have simultaneous second-order localization in both position and frequency. When moving from the lattice translations of Gabor systems to the affine scaling groups of Wavelet bi-frames, how does this obstruction transform, particularly in higher dimensions under anisotropic dilation matrices?

Interactive Figure: Phase-space Heisenberg uncertainty area $\Delta x \cdot \Delta \xi \ge 1/2$. The area of the ellipse is invariant under symplectic transforms, but geometric anisotropy induces directional obstruction.
Key Research Insight

For anisotropic wavelet frames generated by expansive matrices $A \in \text{GL}(d, \mathbb{R})$, the geometric obstruction is characterized by the spectral distribution of the generator matrix and the geometry of the cone singularities in the frequency domain. This connects directly with the weighted Besov decay conditions and sharp Jackson-type estimates.
